TASK: check whether the load generators became the bottleneck of the test. These are the machines that ran the load
testing tool (k6, JMeter, Gatling, Locust), not the system under test.

DOMAIN DATA: the configured queries of the domain, their meaning and units are listed in the "DOMAIN METRICS"
block above; the context sections (sections[]) have the same names as the queries.

ANALYSIS FOCUS:
1. CPU by step: at a utilization above 80–85 % at a step the generator stops delivering the target load and itself
   adds delay to the measured response time. State the step and the per_step.max value; CPU growing faster than the
   load (anomalies.kind=elasticity) is a sign of approaching the limit.
2. Memory: growth within a step under unchanged load (anomalies.kind=drift) or getting close to 100 % is a risk of
   the tool stopping; for JVM tools (JMeter, Gatling) it also means garbage collector pauses.
3. load1 per core above 1 at a step means a CPU queue on the generator, even if the CPU percentage looks moderate.
4. Network: if transmit traffic hits a constant ceiling while the load grows, the generator may have hit the link limit.
5. TCP connections: a sharp rise without load growth is a sign of timeouts and reconnects, not of load on the system.
6. If the generator resources are normal at all steps, say so explicitly: then the test results are not distorted by the generator.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS:
- component — the generator host name from the series name (node=...); if there is none — "unknown".
- In every finding: the step or interval by label and a number from step_table.per_step or top_series.
- Do not draw conclusions about the system under test: only about whether the delivered load and the measured latencies can be trusted.
